Fuel prediction

For every vessel, speed, load and sea state, the predictor estimates daily fuel burn. In this demo a log-linear regression is trained on 3,000 synthetic voyage records with 4% measurement noise, then tested on 800 unseen ones. With real noon reports, AIS tracks and weather feeds, the same slot can hold gradient boosting or a neural network without changing the optimizer.

Quantum-inspired optimization

Each plan is written as 54 bits: nine per voyage, covering vessel, speed level, fuel and shore power. Instead of fixed bits, every position holds a qubit-style angle that gives the probability of being 0 or 1. Each round, every candidate plan is "observed" into a real plan, scored, and its angles are rotated toward the best plan found so far, with occasional NOT-gate flips to keep exploring.

This runs on an ordinary CPU. It borrows the maths of quantum computing but does not use a quantum computer. The progress chart compares it with random search given the same number of tries.

What the optimizer must respect

  • Each vessel serves at most one voyage, and must suit the cargo type and carry the full load.
  • Every voyage meets its deadline, after speed loss in rough seas and current assistance or drag.
  • Each vessel can only burn the fuels its engine supports.
  • Shore power only helps at ports that offer it, and only on vessels that can connect.

Assumptions to replace with real data

  • Fuel prices per tonne: VLSFO $640, LNG $560, bio-methanol $480, green ammonia $650.
  • Net CO₂e per tonne of fuel: VLSFO 3.21, LNG 2.95 (includes methane slip), bio-methanol 0.55, green ammonia 0.
  • Shore power costs 55% of onboard generation fuel cost and emits 30% as much, assuming a mostly renewable supply.
  • Vessel hire, port time (24 hours per call), wave heights and currents are round illustrative numbers.
